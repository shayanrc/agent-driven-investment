Hard-won tuning rules from the nifty50 H=25 hand-driven FS+HP loop (8 configs, `docs/gbdt/_147_nifty50_h25_manual_fs_hp_loop.md`). These are the agent's playbook when *manually* driving the loop (or steering the V1.1 callback) — they encode what the fixed `default_fs_hp_callback` cannot reason about.

**1. Read the train/val gap BEFORE pruning anything.**
- **Why**: the fallback callback prunes on sight, assuming overfit. On nifty50 H=25 the gap was *negative* (val brier < train brier) — **no overfit**. Pruning then only removed capacity → the fallback's blind cut raised val brier (0.1642 → 0.1663). The fallback's instinct was actively wrong.
- **How to apply**: `train_val_gap = val_brier − train_brier`; POSITIVE = val worse than train = overfit (src/gbdt/train.py acts at gap > 0.02). If `train_val_gap ≤ 0.02` (val not meaningfully worse than train) → the model is NOT overfitting; do NOT prune for regularization (at best neutral, usually harmful). **Early-stopping firing is NOT an overfit signal** — it's the healthy mechanism that picks the tree count; a model can early-stop at tree 67 with a deeply negative gap (the nifty50 case). Base the no-overfit call on the gap alone, not on whether early-stop fired.

**2. importance≈0 ≠ unrelated — it usually means REDUNDANT.**
- **Why**: 191/279 features had <0.01 importance, yet 26 of them had a clean monotone relationship with the target; 15 of those were 0.7–0.99 collinear with a *kept* feature. The model needs one vol estimator per window-band, not garman_klass+parkinson+realized_vol (≈0.96–1.00 correlated).
- **How to apply**: don't expect feature selection to *improve* a non-overfit model — removing redundant features is ~neutral on val (nifty50: all-279 0.1642 vs lean-88 0.1650). FS buys leanness/calibration, not accuracy. Verify "is this feature redundant?" via correlation with kept features before concluding it's noise.

**3. HP-ceiling detection — scan depth + lr, then stop.**
- **Why**: across depth {4,6,8} × lr {0.02,0.05}, val brier stayed in [0.1641,0.1661]; R-Precision@10 pinned at ~2.1× throughout (the metric quoted in the original playbook as "R-precision ~2.1×" was the legacy weighted R-precision form — under R-Precision@K the nifty50 H=25 cell lands at 0.368/0.179 = 2.06×, ~2.1× — either way the lesson holds: HP search wasn't moving the needle). No HP config beat the untuned baseline. Burning 8 iterations would have been waste.
- **How to apply**: map the depth response curve (expect an inverted-U; the minimum is the sweet spot — nifty50 was depth 6) and test lr once at the best depth. If the val-brier band across these diverse configs is tiny (≈≤0.002), declare the **HP ceiling** and stop. A clean negative ("no HP headroom") is a valid, valuable result.

**4. Monotone constraints (`monotone_constraints` via `hp_starting`) are neutral-to-harmful on interaction-driven cells — and the marginal-correlation check is a trap.**
- **Why**: nifty50 H=25's signal lives in vol×regime *conditional* interactions. Constraining vol estimators `+1` (chosen by clean *marginal* Spearman) HURT (val 0.1642→0.1664, test AUC 0.733→0.677). Three diagnostics: (a) interaction mass barely moved; (b) 2D PDP showed no sign-flip to destroy; (c) 1D-PDP audit showed 13/17 already monotone in the model, but 4 long-window vol estimators had a learned **inverted-U** (too much vol kills the breakout) the `+1` flattened. Restricting to the 13 already-monotone features STILL lost vs baseline (0.1657) — CatBoost enforces monotonicity at the *tree-structure* level, degrading conditional interactions even when the *net* PDP is monotone. Constraining unused/pruned features = no-op (iter7 ≈ iter6).
- **Two-component harm (iter 8/9 ablation)**: splitting the 17 estimators by interaction involvement and constraining each half alone showed (a) a **fixed ~0.0012 "constraints-on" cost** present even for zero-interaction features (turning on `monotone_constraints` switches CatBoost to a more restrictive tree builder globally), plus (b) a **dominant interaction-specific cost** — constraining just the 8 high-interaction estimators reproduced the *entire* 17-constraint val_brier harm, while removing the low-interaction constraints recovered 0%. There is **no safe subset** to constrain on an interaction-driven cell.
- **How to apply**: (i) marginal monotonicity (Spearman/decile) is NOT sufficient justification — check the **unconstrained model's 1D partial dependence** instead, and even that is necessary-but-not-sufficient. (ii) For an interaction-driven cell (good AUC/R-Precision@K, signal in feature *combinations*), don't bother — constraints range from no-op to harmful, with no safe subset. (iii) `monotone_constraints` does pass through the runner (unknown HP keys flow through `_validate_hp` → `CatBoostClassifier`; named-dict format resolves via Pool feature_names). CatBoost has **no** interaction constraints (XGBoost/LightGBM-only); v1 pins catboost.

**5. The lever for a brier-capped cell with good ranking is OUT of the FS/HP loop.**
- **Why**: nifty50 H=25 ranks well (R-Precision@10 ≈ 2.1× base) but brier improvement is weak and capped by a train→eval **prevalence non-stationarity** (28%→14% positive rate). No FS/HP/constraint touches this — the loop only sees val.
- **How to apply**: when val/eval brier is stuck but AUC/R-Precision@K are strong, suspect a prevalence/regime shift (compare `positive_prevalence` across train/val/eval/test). The fix is recency weighting / regime-conditional calibration (a methodology change), not tuning. Flag it; don't grind iterations against it.

**6. One attributable change per iteration; let results pick the next question.**
- **Why**: each nifty50 iteration held everything fixed except the variable under test, so 8 runs produced a coherent response surface instead of 8 disconnected points. The next experiment was always *generated by* the last result (fast overfit → test shallower; depth settled → test lr; etc.) — the opposite of a fixed heuristic.

**7. `min_child_weight=10` is NOT the universal L2 winner — and there is NO universal recipe across cell shapes.**
- **Why**: r1k trio + _185 + cell-1's first loop all plateaued at `mcw=10` → became the de-facto L2 endpoint. But those were rare-event cells (positive base ≤ 1%, sweep R-p@1 ≈ 0.03 — K=10 was the operating metric, not top-1). On the top-5 cells (sweep R-p@1 0.549–0.800), the L2 grid → mcw=10 regressed loop R-p@1 by **−20% to −32%** vs sweep on cells 1+3+4. Mechanism: mcw=10 caps leaf weights aggressively → smooths the prediction tail → kills the very-high-confidence top picks the sweep was finding via the *un-regularized* baseline. The L1 tie-break (gap + Z) actively selects the most-regularized iter, compounding the harm.
- **How to apply**: don't default `mcw=10` on cells where sweep R-p@1 ≥ 0.5. Treat the L2 grid as a rare-event prescription only. For strong-top-1 cells, START with the iter_0 baseline diagnostics (gap, top features, calibration) and explore per-cell — see rule 9 for the navigation methodology. There is no single recipe that ships for strong-top-1 cells.

**8. Regularization profiles are cell-shape dependent — no transferable recipe.**
- **The cell-4 mix recipe** (`FS to top-30 + mcw=5 + colsample_bytree=0.5 + gamma=0.5`) beat sweep R-p@1 by **+21%** on nasdaq100 +40%/50d (0.549 → 0.667). The same recipe **DESTROYED top-1 on cells 1+3**: cell-1 sp500 +50%/50d (sweep 0.800) → loop_mix 0.360 (**−55% vs sweep, −44% vs the original mcw=10 loop**); cell-3 sp500 +20%/25d (sweep 0.600) → loop_mix 0.240 (**−60% vs sweep**). Mix is **cell-4-specific**, not transferable.
- **Asymmetry hint** (n=3 cells, do not over-read): cells 1+3 are larger panels (sp500, 486 tickers) with strong sweep top-1 (0.6-0.8); cell-4 is smaller (nasdaq100, 92 tickers) with weaker sweep top-1 (0.55). The aggressive FS+stacked-reg on small panels may collapse the diversity the sweep was using to find top picks. Don't generalize the mix to sp500 cells.
- **Knob-effect notes from cell-4** (single-cell evidence, do NOT generalize the values):
  - `mcw` alone (1→5→10): more reg = worse top-1, neutral on K=10
  - `colsample` alone (1.0→0.5): partial top-1 recovery (+16% vs original loop)
  - `gamma` stack (0→0.5→1.0): best K=10/20 recovery (matches sweep)
  - Mix synergy at mcw=5 + cs=0.5 + γ=0.5: top-1 +21% over sweep (cell-4 specific)
  - `mcw=3` on top of mix: top-1 collapses (mcw=5 is the cell-4 sweet spot, not "lower better")
  - `subsample` stacked with `mcw`: catastrophic collapse on common-event cells (cell-2 −87% R-p@1)
  - `depth=8` is no-op when `gamma ≥ 0.5` (gamma already prevents deep splits)
- **How to apply**: the agent's role is to navigate the per-cell response surface, NOT apply a fixed config. Three reliable empirical generalizations across cells: (1) FS rule 2 holds on sp500/nasdaq100 panels — sub-threshold features are usually redundant (FS-only pass is neutral on val_brier); (2) val_brier movement at a fixed knob value does NOT track test ranking shifts (rule 9); (3) the L1 tie-break preferentially selects more-regularized iters that under-perform on test for strong-top-1 cells (the val/test divergence shows the tie-break is calibrated on the wrong objective for these cells).

**9. Single-knob plateau ≠ global plateau — pivot to a structurally-different knob.**
- **Why**: The runner's auto-plateau fires when `val_brier improvement < plateau_threshold` (default 0.005). That means THAT knob plateaued at that value — NOT that all knobs are exhausted. Cell-4: iter_2 (mcw=10) val Δ=0.0012 → runner plateau-stopped → couldn't reach iter_3 to test `colsample` (+16% R-p@1) or `gamma=0.5` (−0.0017 val). Bug #204 filed.
- **Validation-vs-test divergence is real**: cell-4 colsample variant: val_brier moved +0.00001 (noise) between colsample=1.0 and colsample=0.5, but test R-p@1 jumped 0.373 → 0.431. Cell-4 mix iter_1: val 0.023441 (worse than gamma stack's 0.023363), but test R-p@1 0.667 (much better than gamma stack's 0.353). **The val_brier metric is too coarse to detect ranking shifts in the prediction tail.** Don't trust val movement as the sole stop criterion.
- **How to apply**: when a knob value's iter shows small val_brier movement, the agent's decision should **pivot to a structurally-different knob**, NOT call `should_stop=true`. Map a knob's response curve with ≥2 values before pivoting. Knob families to cycle: leaf-weight (`mcw`), column-sample (`colsample_bytree`, `colsample_bylevel`), row-sample (`subsample` — careful on common-event cells), split-quality (`gamma`), structural (`max_depth`), L2 (`reg_lambda`). FS prune is its own axis. When ≥2 structurally-different knob families have plateaued AND test signal isn't moving, call `should_stop`. **Workaround for bug #204 until the runner fix lands**: set `plateau_threshold: 0.0001` in spec to disable auto-plateau in agent_file_protocol mode.

**10. On anti-AUC cells, val_brier has a degenerate global minimum at the constant predictor — and val R-Precision@K is unreliable too. Use eval R-p@K as the holdout oracle.**
- **Why**: cell-5 (nasdaq100 +10%/50d, sweep R-p@1=0.671, sweep test AUC=0.475 in the [0.45, 0.55] null band — the anti-AUC + strong-top-1 case from CLAUDE.md): the booster minimizes val_brier by predicting the weighted base rate everywhere. The gamma curve at γ ≥ 20 (and the alpha curve at α ≥ 30) converges to a degenerate val_brier ≈ 0.226 (the trivial constant predictor's Brier) — the val_brier "winner" has eval R-p@1 = 0.31 (well below baseline). Val R-p@K is also unreliable: Q_days × 1 pick/day is a tiny effective sample, so configs ranked #1 by val R-p@1 landed at eval rank 6, configs ranked #10 landed at eval rank 2, and val_brier was paradoxically a better predictor of eval R-p@1 than val R-p@1 itself. The agent loop's L1 tie-break (gap + Z) actively SELECTS the most-regularized iter in the val_brier band, walking directly into the degenerate sink. See `[[project-fat-tail-eval]]` (eval-segment scoring conventions) and `docs/gbdt/_211_cell5_manual_tuning_xgb.md` for the full evidence.
- **How to apply**: when iter_0 surfaces sweep test AUC ∈ [0.45, 0.55] AND R-Precision@10 lift > 1.5× (CLAUDE.md anti-AUC anti-pattern), turn on **per-iter eval R-p@K scoring** alongside val signals and treat eval R-p@K as the oracle when val signals disagree. Specifically, when val_brier descends but eval R-p@1 stays flat or drops, you're approaching the degenerate sink — pivot to a structurally-different knob or call `should_stop`. The runner doesn't surface per-iter eval predictions today (predictions only emitted at finalization) — for now, drive the loop manually per rule 11 with an off-loop scoring script (see `docs/gbdt/_211_*` for the recipe). **Scope**: this eval-oracle rule is the in-loop 3-window setup (train/val/eval, eval = the terminal holdout). For a **canonical-period finetune** (5-window, with a separate held-out `test`), eval INVERTS vs test on these cells — do NOT treat eval as the oracle; select on val, confirm on `test` (judge the test book), per `docs/gbdt/CANONICAL_FINETUNE_RECIPE.md`.

**11. The FS → response-curve → mix-and-match → eval-validation manual methodology is reusable across cells where the loop's val_brier objective is misaligned.**
- **Why**: cell-5 manual-tuning track beat sweep R-p@1 by **+24%** (test R-p@1 0.8286 vs 0.6714) using 8 batches of standalone scripts — about ~15 min wall-clock end-to-end on hist+nj=8 + cached features. The agent loop never finds this regime because (a) its val_brier objective is wrong here (rule 10) and (b) its default HP envelope (eta=0.3 + no ES + n_estimators=large) never tries the tiny-model regime that wins (rule 12).
- **The five-stage recipe**: (1) **FS cliff cut** — drop importance < 0.01 features (byte-identical model under colsample=1.0 but cleaner state); (2) **single-knob response curves** — sweep each HP family across 3–5 values, identify non-degenerate sweet spots, FLAG knobs that descend monotonically to a degenerate val_brier sink (gamma/alpha typically do); (3) **mix-and-match** — combine structurally different knob families at moderate non-degenerate values; (4) **eval-segment validation** — score the top val candidates on eval R-p@K too; if eval ordering differs from val ordering, the val signals are misaligned (re-rank on eval); (5) **1-shot TEST shootout** — score the top eval candidates ONCE on test to commit. Each fit is 3–10 s on hist+nj=8 + cached features.
- **How to apply**: use this recipe when (a) rule 10 is triggered, (b) the agent loop's first pass under-performs versus sweep on the operating metric, or (c) you suspect an HP regime the loop's defaults can't reach. Use `scripts/gbdt/regenerate_r_precision_at_k_csv.py` for canonical R-Precision@K scoring or replicate the formula inline (`(1/Q) · Σ_q r_q / min(K, R_q)` with `(p_calibrated desc, ticker asc)` stable mergesort tie-break per `[[project-r-precision-methodology]]`).
- **V1.3 Option B codifies stages 1-3 of this recipe** as Phase 1.4-1.6 of `/gbdt-experiment` (`docs/gbdt/V1.3_OPTION_B_PLAN.md`; opt in via `backend.scout.enabled: true` + `backend.fs_prefit.enabled: true`). Phase 1.4 = FS-prefit cliff cut (stage 1); Phase 1.5 = 8 single-knob response curves × ~4-5 values (stage 2); Phase 1.6 = combine (stage 3) — either the runner's lex auto-compose (default mode; oracle `R-p@1 > 3 > 5 > 10 > 20`) or the agent's mix-and-match decision (agent_file_protocol mode, ≤ 50 mix configs via the `scout/combine_request.json` → `combine_decision.json` → `combine_results.json` → `iter_0_decision.json` exit-resume cycles). Stages 4-5 (eval-validation + test shootout) are NOT codified — they remain the manual post-loop step, since they consume the test segment which the loop's eval-vs-test discipline forbids touching at training time.

**12. Tiny models can dominate big ones on top-1 — don't dismiss configs whose val_brier is near baseline.**
- **Why**: cell-5 winner is a **6-tree, max_depth=2 XGBoost** (≤ 24 leaves total) with `eta=0.1 + early_stopping_rounds=30 + colsample_bytree=0.4 + scale_pos_weight=1.0 + gamma=0 + FS=top-130`. Its val_brier is 0.2244 — RIGHT at the weighted-base-rate baseline of 0.2259, so most predictions are at the prior, with only 6 trees of small perturbations carrying the signal. Standard "val_brier close to baseline = model didn't learn" intuition is wrong on anti-AUC cells: the prediction-tail shape is what matters for R-p@1, not the bulk Brier. Sweep's CatBoost-default (1000 iters, depth 6, ~64,000 max leaves) gets test R-p@1 = 0.671 on the same cell; the 24-leaf model gets 0.829. Bigger does not mean better here.
- **How to apply**: on anti-AUC + strong-top-1 cells, explicitly try the tiny-model regime: `max_depth ∈ {2, 3}` + `eta ∈ {0.05, 0.1}` + early stopping + `colsample_bytree ∈ {0.3, 0.4, 0.5}`. Score on eval R-p@K (rule 10) — val_brier may stay near baseline because the model IS mostly the prior, and that's correct on this cell shape. The win is in the reliability table's prediction-extreme bin (val bin > 0.6 with high `frac_positive`), not in bulk Brier.

**13. If the XGBoost agent-tune doesn't beat the cell's sweep, try a CatBoost agent-tune next — it's the best agent variant and occasionally wins big, but it's high-variance, so validate it against the sweep before trusting it.**
- **Why**: across the H=200/H=100 cbagent extension (5 cells, `_269`/`_270`/`_271`), switching the `agent_file_protocol` backend from XGBoost → CatBoost was the single most reliable upgrade to the *agent* result — the CatBoost agent beat the XGBoost agent at @1 on 4/5 cells, and on one cell beat the *sweep* spectacularly (sp500 +50%/200d: test R-p@1 **0.930** vs sweep 0.630, +0.30). But it did **NOT** reliably beat the sweep: 1 clear win (sp500/50), 1 marginal mixed (russell/50 #1: +0.007 @1 / −0.055 @3), 2 @1-losses-but-@3-wins (sp500/40, russell/40-200d), 1 clear loss (russell/40/100d: −0.100 @1 / −0.108 @3). The outcome is set entirely by **eval→test decay direction** — all 5 had eval R-p@1 ≈ 0.80–0.93 yet test R-p@1 ranged 0.45→0.93 — which the agent cannot see, so the headline is a coin-flip, not a dependable gain. The scout auto-translates XGBoost-vocab HP → CatBoost (`max_depth→depth`, `eta→learning_rate`, `colsample_bytree→rsm`, `min_child_weight→min_data_in_leaf`, `alpha→l2_leaf_reg`; `gamma` is dropped — no CatBoost analog), so the same response-curve playbook applies.
- **How to apply**: when an XGBoost agent-tune under-performs the cell's CatBoost sweep, run a CatBoost agent-tune (`backend.library: catboost`, same scout + fs_prefit + agent_file_protocol) as the next move — worth it for the occasional big win and it's the strongest agent config. Three guardrails: (i) **always validate the CatBoost-agent pick against the sweep on the TEST window** — resnap the sweep on the current vintage if it's `_266`-faithfulness-suspect (the sweep reproduces to the decimal, so the resnapped bar is trustworthy); the ≤3-iter CatBoost sweep stays the deployed default unless the agent decisively beats it at the operating K. (ii) Prefer the **broad-not-@1-spiky combine config** (L2-regularized, not feature-subsampled): the spiky eval-@1 winners decay hardest, and aggressive FS/subsample gives up the @2–@3 band — but note that band loss is an HP/ranking-shape effect, NOT a feature deficit (`_270` #26: the 53-feat FS subset retained 92.8% of the sweep's importance mass + all its top-20 features, yet still demoted #2–3 picks into #4–5). (iii) **Don't chase eval R-p@1** — at H≥100 it is an unreliable single-window proxy for *both* backends; the binding constraint is the eval objective (multi-window / decay-penalized, V1.5_TBD #2), not the backend. A negative result is valid and expected roughly half the time — report it, don't force a win.

**14. FS-first is MANDATORY the moment a finetune applies any smoothing/regularizing HP change. Rules 1-2 ("don't prune a non-overfit model; FS is ~neutral") describe FS-as-accuracy-lever at the CURRENT (usually default) HP — they do NOT license skipping FS when you also move HP toward a smoother model.**
- **Why**: On nasdaq100 +50%/25d vwap (a no-overfit, rare-event cell — gap 0.005), driving the loop with a slow-learn+ES schedule (eta 0.05, early_stopping, depth 4) on the FULL 293-feature pool **catastrophically anti-selected the test top-of-book**: R-p@3 0.716 (single-fit) → **0.055**. The SAME schedule with a cliff-cut FS-first (drop importance < 0.01, or |r| > 0.9-redundant) recovered R-p@3 → **0.693** (competitive) + beat the single-fit on AUC/@10/@20. FS-only at default HP was byte-identical to the single-fit (rule 2 holds — redundant features are inert under the greedy default). They only turn HARMFUL under the smoothing schedule: low eta + many shallow trees spread splits into the redundant / large-scale-noise families (esp. the F16 `*_outside_band` persistence family, std up to 200+), diluting the sharp top-tail ranking. FS is inert at default HP but protective under smoothing.
- **How to apply**: whenever a finetune iteration lowers `eta` / adds `early_stopping_rounds` / lowers `max_depth` / raises any regularizer, **cliff-cut the sub-floor (importance < 0.01) + correlation-redundant (|r| > 0.9 with a higher-importance feature) features in the SAME or a PRIOR iteration — never apply the smoothing on the full pool.** Use `backend.fs_prefit.enabled: true` (the codified Phase-1.4 cliff-cut) OR a manual `prune_features` cliff-cut in the iter_0 decision. Applies to ALL cells, rare AND common — the anti-selection was WORST on the rare cell, so the pre-2026-07-08 "leave fs_prefit OFF on rare-event cells" advice is REVERSED for the FS-prefit component (the scout HP-grid is a separate opt-in). The corr-based (26 feats) and the cliff-cut (161 feats) prunes captured ~identical benefit (both R-p@3 0.693), so the simple cliff-cut suffices.
- **Caveat — FS bounds the downside, it does not guarantee a win.** Rare-event / long-horizon top-of-book stays noise-dominated: test R-p@3 swings 0.05–0.72 across *neighboring* HP configs, and NO in-loop signal predicts it (eval R-p@1 ↔ test is inverted/random on rare cells; predictive on common ones). Where the agent-finetune DOES help robustly: **common event (prevalence ≳ 15%) + mid horizon (≤ ~50d) + a real overfit the schedule fully closes (gap → < 0.02)** — there it lifts @3–@20 + AUC (nasdaq100 +20%/50d fund: every K up, @3 +0.06). Long horizon (H ≥ 100, rule-13 decay) or a noisy feature arm breaks it even when common. Reliably across cells the finetune lifts the mid/broad book (@5–@10); the sharp @1 is the fragile part.
